5 Common JAMB Maths Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

JAMB maths is timed and multiple-choice, which means small habits cost real marks. Here are the five mistakes that come up again and again, and how to fix each one.

1. Rushing straight to the options

It's tempting to glance at the four options and guess which "looks right." Solve the question properly first, then match your answer, don't let the options guide your working.

2. Sign errors when moving terms across the equals sign

When a term crosses the equals sign, its sign flips. Forgetting this is one of the single biggest sources of wrong answers in algebra questions. Write out the flip as its own step instead of doing it in your head.

3. Misreading fractions in the question

3½ and 3/½ are very different numbers. Under exam pressure, it's easy to misread a mixed number as a division. Slow down for one extra second on any question with a fraction.

4. Not simplifying the final answer

If you get 8/4 but the options only show 2, you haven't finished the question, you've just stopped solving it. Always simplify or convert your answer to match the form the options are given in.

5. Forgetting the units

Time, currency, and measurement questions often mix units (minutes vs seconds, days vs hours). Convert everything to the same unit before you start calculating, not after.

Exam tip: If you finish a question and none of the four options match, don't just pick the closest one, redo the last two steps. A mismatch almost always means a small, fixable slip, not a wrong method.
